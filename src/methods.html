Method & sources · Version 1.0.0

What the numbers mean

ExetatGPA adds cohort-relative standing to an official EXETAT score. The percentage and examination session are preserved. Positions are calculated from recovered published successful scores in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, separately for each session from 2008 through 2023.

1. Position among published successful scores

For your score s, let a be the number of recovered published successful scores at least as high as s, and let n be the total recovered published successful-score count in that session. The upper-tail position is a/n. All ties are included. “Top 2.59%” means that 2.59% of these recovered published scores are at least as high as the selected score. It does not establish a national percentile.

Only actually observed integer scores are accepted. There are 648 observed session–score pairs. A further 28 thresholds in the 676 year–score reference profiles had no recovered exact observation and are withheld by this app. Scores below 50, scores above the observed maximum, absent integer scores, decimal scores and sessions outside 2008–2023 are unavailable.

2. Correspondence across EXETAT sessions

The source upper-tail position is located in the target session’s recovered published successful-score distribution. Linear interpolation is applied between adjacent supported integer scores. A result is withheld when the position falls beyond the target’s observed tail range; no extrapolation is used. Thus, 59% in 2021 corresponds to 61.38% in 2022 in cohort-relative standing. This is not a prediction of an individual’s score in 2022 or evidence of equal attainment.

3. Eight separate external references

Each reference retains its own examination, session, population and published resolution. The EXETAT upper-tail position is mapped into each distribution separately. No composite international grade is calculated.

  1. United States. Reported GPA categories among 1,510,132 GPA respondents in the 2016 pre-March SAT graduating-senior report, tested through January 2016. Latest questionnaire responses were used and students were counted once. The six categories are A+, A, A-minus, B, C and D/E/F. Exact frequencies, accumulated strongest to weakest, determine the category. An exact cumulative boundary belongs to the category ending at that boundary. No numerical GPA or category interpolation is used. College Board source, Table 12.
  2. France. Candidates of all registration statuses in the June 2025 general baccalaureate. Frequency-based linear interpolation gives the final average on the 0–20 scale, displayed to one decimal. Published rounded percentages total approximately 100.0003%. Ministry distribution.
  3. Japan. 2024 Common Test main-session Mathematics I/A examinees. The empirical step inverse preserves integer marks on the 0–100 subject scale. It is not a whole-examination result. National Center for University Entrance Examinations.
  4. South Korea. 2024 CSAT Mathematics area examinees. The empirical step inverse retains the standard-score distribution; published thresholds determine the corresponding grade band. Standard scores are not raw percentages. Ministry frequency distribution.
  5. England. Summer 2024 A-level entries across all subjects. The empirical step inverse preserves A*–U categories. These are subject entries, not overall student grades. JCQ source.
  6. Germany. National 2024 Abitur final-grade frequencies across 16 states. Lower grades indicate stronger outcomes, so frequencies are accumulated from the lowest grade. The step inverse preserves grade resolution. Candidates without a passing result are shown as “Not passed.” KMK distribution.
  7. Türkiye. 2024 YKS Basic Proficiency Test (TYT) candidates with a published examination score of at least 100. The empirical step inverse preserves 20-point threshold bands at the published band resolution. ÖSYM statistics.
  8. South Africa. 2024 National Senior Certificate Mathematics performance bands. The empirical step inverse preserves ten-point percentage bands, using frequencies published to one decimal. Department of Basic Education report.

Eligible distributions were not retained for Canada, China, India, Brazil or New Zealand. No numerical conversion is offered for those destinations.

4. Bounds for unpublished EXETAT scores

The broader denominator comprises participants represented in the same recovered groups as the published scores. If N is that participant count and U = N − n participants have no published exact score, the proportion at least as high as your score is bounded by:

a / N ≤ proportion at or above s ≤ (a + U) / N

The lower limit assigns all unpublished scores below the threshold; the upper limit assigns them all at or above it. No distributional assumption is imposed. Each endpoint is mapped separately into every external reference. These are missing-score bounds within the represented-participant frame, not confidence intervals, national ranks or measures of sampling uncertainty. They do not resolve errors, omissions or selection in the recovered corpus.

5. US reference nonresponses remain separate

The SAT report also lists 127,457 students with no GPA-category response. For target-reference sensitivity, all are assigned to either A+ or D/E/F. The app shows this target-only span with the published EXETAT position fixed, and a combined source-and-target span. This bounds allocations among the six reported categories, not unobserved numerical GPA. No comparable nonresponse recalculation is asserted for the other seven references.

Worked example

70% in EXETAT 2023 occupies the top 2.59% among 759,819 recovered published successful scores. Its matched outputs are US A+; France 17.4/20; Japan 91/100; Korean Mathematics standard score 136, grade 1; England A*; German Abitur 1.0; Türkiye TYT 400–419; and South African Mathematics 80–89%. The US source-bound span is A to A+, and remains A to A+ when source bounds and the limiting target nonresponse allocations are combined.

Provenance and reproducibility

The app uses aggregate score counts and published reference distributions. Individual examination records and names are not included. File hashes, source links and mapping rules are documented in the provenance manifest. The aggregate EXETAT histogram, seven reference distributions, US category frequencies and eight-reference worked example can be inspected. Executable calculations and verification tests are available on GitHub.

The percentile-translation lineage is described by Mateos Aparicio, Hernández and Martínez (2016). The implementation describes distributional correspondence. Equivalent attainment, individual counterfactual scores, credential equivalence and demonstrated admissions benefit are not established.

Privacy

Your selected score is processed locally in the browser. The app has no accounts, input telemetry, persistent storage or third-party calculation service. Hosting receives ordinary page requests. No score is included in the URL or sent to the host.
